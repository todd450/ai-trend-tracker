What was said

Watched 13 Nov, in part

Day one was about running AI agents in bulk rather than one at a time. Speakers described “software factories”: many agents writing, checking and merging code, with people setting the rules and deciding how far to trust them. A second theme was small, fast “decision” models that answer yes-or-no questions in a fraction of a second, used beside the large models to cut cost and to check agents’ work. Day two turned to what an organisation has to change to absorb that much code: disposable test environments, tests that exist before the code does, review that keeps pace, and people who still decide what is worth building. Against the tracker, agents took about two thirds of topic mentions across both days while their research and discussion counts are roughly flat; tool use was loud and is rising in discussion; coding tools were loud here and flat in the numbers. Safety was rarely named, though much of both days was about verifying what agents do.

What was and was not watched

Day one: automatic captions of the day-one stream (11 November), read in full, about nine hours: the morning and closing keynotes and the session and online talks shown between them. Day two (12 November) was read, not watched: the organiser’s summary of each of its 23 sessions and its longer written edition for eight of them, both published on the organiser’s site; the recording was not played.
